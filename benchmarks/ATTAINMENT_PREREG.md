Two questions from the owner's review of Entry 17, frozen before either is answered in code.

## A — the attainment hierarchy

The owner's four levels, and the claim that a serious system must say which one it has earned:

    1  NO CURRENT OBJECTIVE      no represented investigation is presently justified
    2  FRONTIER EXHAUSTED        every represented investigation is resolved or blocked
    3  NAMED COVERAGE EXHAUSTED  a predeclared challenge found no defect over failure classes whose
                                 coverage was independently established
    4  COMPLETE                  no important failure exists anywhere

Legasus reports QUIESCENT_CONTEST, which is level 1 and is silent about 2 and 3. The levels are not
the same claim, and reporting the weakest while sounding like the strongest is the Entry 16 error
with more resolution.

    AT-1  MEASURED already, restated as a prediction: of the 8 represented investigations, 7 carry a
          block class that is RESOLVED or a genuine block (PLATFORM, TERMINAL, SEQUENCING, EPISTEMIC,
          OWNER) and exactly 1 - INSTRUMENT_CLASSES_FOR_THE_RIGS - is UNKNOWN-classed, which is
          NEITHER resolved NOR blocked. Therefore LEVEL 2 IS NOT EARNED.
    AT-2  Level 3 is not earned either, and for an independently recorded reason: instruments.mjs
          reports the rigs' failure classes as UNKNOWN, and level 3 requires established coverage.
          The composition waves were adversarial development, not a predeclared coverage procedure.
    AT-3  Level 4 is not representable and the function must refuse to compute it rather than return
          false - "no important failure exists anywhere" is not a proposition this system can hold.
    AT-4  CONTROL. A hypothetical frontier whose every entry is resolved-or-blocked DOES earn level 2,
          so the computation discriminates and is not a machine that always says 1.
    AT-5  CONTROL. Attainment changes no verdict: the contest state, the objectives list and every
          block class are what they were.

## B — does mechanizing an instance mechanize the class?

THE SESSION'S OWN COUNTEREXAMPLE. C4 - *a coordinate acquired a declared dimension's authority
because two strings matched* - was found and repaired at `a31046c`. At `58b62aa`, four commits later,
the C8 repair exported `CONTESTED` from provenance.mjs, colliding with `ledger.mjs`'s STATE.CONTESTED
whose LAW 3 meaning carries an obligation - and the obligation traveled. Same class, mechanized,
recurred.

The reason is visible in the repair: C4's guard is producer-keyed promotion INSIDE justification.mjs.
It guards one mechanism, not the class. The ledger called it a class anyway.

    AT-6  MEASURED, restated: across legaknow's non-test modules there are 97 distinct exported state
          words and exactly 7 owned by more than one module (SPECIFICATION, OWNER, UNKNOWN,
          ASSUMPTION, INVALID, PROOF, REPLACED). A small enumerable set, not noise.
    AT-7  THE DEMONSTRATED TRUE POSITIVE. Adding `CONTESTED` to provenance.mjs - the exact defect of
          58b62aa - makes it the 8th collision. A guard that freezes the inventory and reports any
          NEW collision would have fired at the moment the defect was introduced. To be proven by
          reconstruction, not by argument.
    AT-8  AND THE HONEST LIMIT. None of the 7 standing collisions is a demonstrated defect: they are
          namespaced by their enum (VALIDITY.INVALID vs LIFECYCLE.INVALID) and no obligation has been
          shown to travel between them. So the guard must NOT claim to detect danger. What made
          CONTESTED dangerous was that the colliding word carried an OBLIGATION, and which words carry
          obligations is not mechanically derivable - the same hand-authored boundary instruments.mjs
          already records. The guard therefore surfaces a new collision for ARGUMENT and classifies
          nothing.

## What this buys, stated so it is not oversold

Not "the class can no longer recur". A new collision becomes visible AT INTRODUCTION instead of four
commits later in someone else's review. That is the owner's compounding property in its weak,
achievable form - absorb a discovery so the next instance is cheaper to find - and explicitly not the
strong form, which this session has a counterexample to.
