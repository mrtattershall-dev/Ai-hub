Repair C2 and C3: the calculus reads absence the way the graph does, and the brand is no longer readable off a token

C2. derive() filtered absent values out before intersecting, so {} joined with {repository: S1} minted
a conclusion AT S1 - a premise that licensed nothing over repository lent the conclusion the other
premise's world (b11e51f, C2-a). That read absence as "for all" while justification.mjs reads the
same absence as "never established, licenses nothing": two readings of one thing, the domain-algebra
defect again. A dimension now enters the output only when every premise establishes it and they
agree; anything else is DROPPED and the drop is recorded in the ancestry. A drop, not a refusal: a
conclusion may be weaker than its premises. A bridged conflict is likewise recorded as dropped.

C3. The brand was a Symbol key on the token, readable with Object.getOwnPropertySymbols; a frozen
object carrying it, a NORMATIVE kind, an ungranted grant and an ancestry entry naming OWNER passed
isAuthority(), tracesToIndependentRoot() and commit() (b11e51f, C3-a). Membership in a module-private
WeakSet replaces it: nothing is on the object to copy, a spread, a clone and a JSON round-trip are
anonymous objects, and the only way in is token(), which only the constructors call. invalidate()
now mints through token() so an invalidated token is still recognisably a token.

Stated plainly rather than as a security claim: the calculus has no production consumer (measured
at b11e51f), so nothing at runtime asks isAuthority() before acting. This closes a representational
hole; it protects no path that does not ask.

Admit controls executed: agreeing premises keep their dimension, a bridged conflict drops with a
record, delegate/narrow/restrictGrant/invalidate all still yield tokens, and the FREEZE-GATE's ten
refusals and nine admissions are unchanged.
