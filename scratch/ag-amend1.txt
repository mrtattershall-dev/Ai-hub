ACTION-GOVERNANCE-1 amendment 1: the delegation must bind, and DELEGATE is the only constructor with no context discipline

Frozen BEFORE any build and before any result exists. Nothing in ACTION-GOVERNANCE-1 has been implemented
or run, so this is an amendment to a preregistration rather than a revision after an outcome.

MEASURED BASELINE rather than assumed. Of the eight bindings a delegation should carry, TWO ARE ALREADY
ENFORCED and six are absent:

    principal / root          ENFORCED   from must be OWNER or an existing authority token
    action type               ENFORCED   grant non-empty; tracesToIndependentRoot refuses uncovered need
    target scope              ABSENT     context || {} - optional, unvalidated, never checked
    evidence preconditions    ABSENT     no link from an admitted certificate to a grant
    time / run binding        ABSENT     zero expiry/issuedAt/ttl in the calculus
    budget / blast radius     ABSENT     zero budget
    validation requirement    ABSENT     commit() checks authority, not test outcomes
    revocation                ABSENT     zero revoke

AND THE ACTUAL FINDING IS AN ASYMMETRY, narrower than "delegation needs constraints". The calculus applies
a STRICT context discipline to its epistemic constructors and NONE to the one that creates permission:

    OBSERVE    REFUSES without a context
    DERIVE     INTERSECTS premise contexts - output can only SHRINK
    DELEGATE   context || {} - optional, never compared to the action

The constructor that mints normative authority is the only one allowed an empty or arbitrary context. A
grant is scoped by WHAT may be done and not by WHERE, TO WHAT, FOR HOW LONG, or ON WHAT EVIDENCE.

THE CENTRAL CAUTION IS ALREADY STRUCTURALLY HELD, recorded so it is not re-solved: a controller cannot
mint a root-traceable token. delegate() refuses unless from is literally OWNER or an existing authority,
and refuses a non-NORMATIVE grantor with "Evidence establishes what may be BELIEVED; it does not originate
permission." An epistemic certificate cannot be laundered into permission by re-delegation. Any change
that lets a controller-held token satisfy from is a REGRESSION, and A5 exists to catch it.

SEVEN NEW PREDICTIONS, A8-A14, every one a refusal condition and every one a first-class success: scope
mismatch, replay, budget, revocation, wrong root, validation failure, and A14 CONTEXT MONOTONICITY - a
delegated context may only NARROW, mirroring DERIVE's intersection. A14 is the one I expect to need real
design rather than a check, and without it the other six are defeatable by re-delegating with a wider
context.

Plus the outcome taxonomy: thirteen named outcomes, so a run retaining ZERO repairs is still interpretable,
and a run whose only outcome is ACTION_PERMITTED is explicitly NOT a success - the positive control must be
accompanied by a denial of each declared class. Every denial must fire FOR ITS OWN REASON under an injected
fault; a refusal caused by a missing import, an absent fixture, or a wrong working directory is not a
governance result, and this project has produced all three and mistaken them for findings.
