Entry 19: the ledger's own vocabulary was overclaiming, and the compounding thesis is narrowed to what survived

This ledger has been writing mechanized for a demonstrated region and reading it as a covered class.
That is how C4 got four commits of cover, and at scale it is how a catalogue of named failures reads
as a guarantee. The three claims are now representable, and the first thing represented was this
project's own case: C4 earns MECHANIZED_REGION at two of two observed incidents, with the ratio
printed beside the verdict rather than as it, because known incidents are a sample.

The fact that hid the overclaim is that the two detectors are disjoint. The inventory guard cannot
see the original C4 at all, since collectionCohort was a runtime data key and not an exported state
word - measured rather than assumed. So the pattern is covered only as a union of disjoint regions,
which is not a claim about the pattern. And the distance from introducing the CONTESTED defect to
recording it was twenty commits containing seven passing full suites, which is the 452/452 shape at
small scale.

The compounding thesis is restated at the strength that survived: not that every failure makes the
system unable to repeat it, which this session falsified in four commits, but that every discovery
should leave behind reusable protection whose exact coverage is known, so repetitions inside that
coverage become cheaper. For C4 the answer is honestly mixed - the first repair did not cover the
provenance route, the accumulated machinery did make the recurrence recognizable, and there is now a
detector that would have surfaced that specific cross-module form at introduction.

One data point is reported as one data point. The owner named a family of long-run metrics and no
framework is built over n equals one, which would be the budget-becomes-entitlement error in a new
costume. History is not rewritten; entries 15 to 18 keep the word they used.
