Characterise what survives a parent's death on this box: DETACHMENT decides it, not stdio - and my taskkill hypothesis is falsified

Taken up at BIND-1's offer: runLifecycle.test.mjs hit its 180s budget, taskkill /T /F fired, and close
took ~50 MORE MINUTES. Something holding stdio outlived the tree kill.

MY HYPOTHESIS, stated first and now DEAD: taskkill /T walks the CURRENT parent-pid chain, so a child
whose parent already exited is unreachable from the root and survives. It never got tested, because the
apparatus could not construct the state, and the reason it could not is the finding.

TWO APPARATUS DEFECTS OF MINE, both recorded rather than quietly fixed.
  1. The first version spawned mid.mjs AS the root, so root and mid were ONE process - the output printed
     the same pid for both. In the exit variant the root therefore died before the kill and taskkill was
     aimed at an already-dead pid. It printed "hypothesis FALSIFIED"; that reading was unsupported and is
     withdrawn. A THREE-level tree is required for the question to be asked at all.
  2. A node -e probe lost its regex to shell escaping, so the leaf pid parsed as null and tasklist was
     asked for "PID eq null" four times. That is my own recorded rule - never pass prose through a shell
     argument - broken for the second time today. Rewritten as a file.

The repaired experiment then REFUSED TO REPORT, which is the apparatus working: its precondition check
found the leaf already dead before the kill, so the orphan state never existed and no reading of the kill
is admissible either way. That refusal left the sharper question, because 263 witness processes WERE
observed alive with "(parent gone)" earlier today. Both cannot be true without a distinguishing condition.

MEASURED, with the leaf reporting its pid through a FILE rather than the pipe under test:

    plain     stdio inherited      leaf DIES with its parent
    detached  detached + unref     leaf SURVIVES
    piped     stdio piped          leaf DIES with its parent

DETACHMENT DECIDES IT. Inherited versus piped stdio makes no difference at all - which kills the "it was
holding the pipe" framing as the explanation for survival, though not as the explanation for close
staying open.

SCOPE, AND IT MATTERS: this measures a parent EXITING NORMALLY, not a parent FORCE-KILLED. taskkill /F is
not process.exit(0) and the two may differ. So this does not yet explain BIND-1's 50 minutes; it removes
one candidate and names the variable that actually governs survival.
