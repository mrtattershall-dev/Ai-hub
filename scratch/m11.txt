THREE OUTCOMES, none inherently bad: PARALLEL_SAFE, SERIALIZATION_REQUIRED, PROCESS_ISOLATION_REQUIRED.
The experiment determines the authority envelope; it has no preferred answer.

NON-VACUITY IS STRICT. Overlap may NOT be inferred from Promise.all, timestamps or completion. Both
subjects carry a barrier and the run counts as a parallelism test only on a MUTUAL WITNESS - A saw B
entered and B saw A entered, each before release. Absent that the outcome is OVERLAP_NOT_ESTABLISHED,
which is not a pass.

FOUR PROPERTIES ATTACKED INDEPENDENTLY: protocol integrity, attribution, NON-INTERFERENCE (the dangerous
one - perfect attribution can be built around an environment that already changed the program being
measured), and POST-FAILURE ISOLATION (a hostile run must not contaminate the next one while the later
record looks pristine).

THE ATTACK THAT MATTERS: subject A manipulates fd 1 WHILE B IS ALIVE - close, dup2, fork with inherited
descriptors, writes after the window. If that defeats isolation the finding is stronger than "dup2 needs
a lock": the subject possesses causal authority over the same process-global resource the observer relies
on for evidence custody, and a mutex can only schedule access to that mismatch, not repair it.

MY PRIOR, STATED SO IT CAN BE WRONG: runIsolated spawns a process per observation, so I EXPECT the
attacks to fail and the API to be serialized by construction. A prediction I believe is exactly the kind
that needs the strict overlap witness.

NOT BEING DECIDED HERE: whether to move to worker processes. The current design must be made to FAIL
first, or process isolation becomes architecture motivated by expectation rather than evidence.

Also recorded: the 2/5 OUTPUT_MISMATCH naming issue sits on the REPLAY path, which the external-producer
boundary supersedes. Maintaining two implementations of doctest semantics would recreate the problem just
eliminated, so it is a RETIREMENT candidate rather than a repair candidate.
