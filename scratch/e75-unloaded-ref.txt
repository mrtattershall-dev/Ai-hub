Capture the unloaded calibration reference while the machine is verifiably quiet - and one result against my own advice

0d cleared 261 leaked processes; the sampler confirms 9 node processes at 18:55:03Z. A verified-quiet box
is the scarce resource here, so the reference was taken immediately rather than deferred.

MEASURED, JIT warmed, 5 runs each:

    BIND-1 workload 2e8    853 808 856 950 958 ms    min 808.5   max 957.8   spread 1.185
    short workload 1.2e7   43.3 54.4 59.6 45.4 47.0  min  43.3   max  59.6   spread 1.377

THE TRUE UNLOADED BASELINE IS ~808ms, NOT THE ~1500ms MEASURED UNDER LOAD. That quantifies the anchoring
problem raised earlier: the 1500ms figure was ~1.85x inflated, so a cap set at 3x-of-loaded is 5.6x of
true unloaded; and a per-attempt baseline captured on a box permanently at 1500ms reads 1.0x forever,
leaving the ratio rule blind to exactly the state this machine was in for most of today.

AND ONE FINDING AGAINST MY OWN ADVICE, reported because it is against it. I recommended cutting the
calibration workload to 50-100ms. The short workload is NOISIER in relative terms - 1.377 against 1.185 -
so "shorter is strictly better" was wrong; it trades precision for self-load. It remains the right trade,
and now with numbers instead of assertion: 1.377 against a 3.0 threshold leaves comfortable headroom, and
43ms against 808ms is ~19x less self-load, which is what stops two abort-on-breach runners aborting each
other indefinitely.

Warmup is not optional. A cold-JIT first sample is an outlier that inflates a baseline and then HIDES REAL
LOAD BEHIND IT.

Conditions are recorded alongside the numbers deliberately, so a later reader can tell what they were
taken in rather than inheriting them as context-free constants.
