taskkill /T /F DOES reach a live non-detached grandchild - so BIND-1's 50-minute stdio holder was not its own descendant

Question (c) as BIND-1 specified it, with both outcomes preregistered by them: leaf survives means /T is
not walking to it and record-every-pid-and-sweep is the only bound; leaf dies means the holder came from
another lineage. The answer is the second.

MEASURED, three-level tree, all alive at the moment of the kill, none detached, and the precondition that
all three are alive asserted before any reading is admitted:

    grandchild stdio PIPED     (their hub configuration)   root/mid/leaf all DEAD after /T /F
    grandchild stdio INHERIT                               root/mid/leaf all DEAD after /T /F

AND THE EVIDENCE IS DIRECT RATHER THAN INFERRED - taskkill names what it walked to:

    SUCCESS: The process with PID 39392 (child process of PID 38124) has been terminated.
    SUCCESS: The process with PID 38124 (child process of PID 23372) has been terminated.
    SUCCESS: The process with PID 23372 (child process of PID  5632) has been terminated.

The grandchild is named explicitly, as a child of the mid, so the walk reaches two levels down. A corpse
count alone would have left open whether the leaf died from the kill or from losing its parent; the
walk's own output does not.

TAKEN WITH THE EARLIER PROBE the picture closes: a non-detached child dies when its parent exits, a
detached one survives, and /T /F reaches live non-detached descendants at least two levels deep. BIND-1
reports no detached:true anywhere under server/. THEREFORE THE HOLDER WAS NOT A DESCENDANT OF THE ROOT AT
ALL, and no better kill of that tree would have helped - which means the fix is identification, not force.
