E1 revision binding and E3 evidence obligations: 27/27, and the positive control caught a fixture that made a refusal meaningless

E1 - REVISION BINDING, 4/4. A grant issued against revision A, the target then becomes revision B, the
edit is attempted:
    E1-ALLOWED    unchanged revision, the positive control: proceeds to the exact bytes
    E1-DENIED     ACTION_DENIED_REVISION_MISMATCH naming both revisions, and B's BYTES ARE PRESERVED -
                  not overwritten with the proposal, and NOT REVERTED TO A. Refusing is not repairing.
    E1-CONTROL    identical authority, action and root across two runs; the ONLY difference is whether the
                  file moved underneath, so the denial is caused by the revision alone
    E1-SCOPE      an authority pinning NO revision is unaffected - recorded as a deliberate conditional
                  rather than an omission, so tightening it later is a visible policy change
The revision is the DIGEST OF THE CURRENT BYTES read at the execution boundary, not an mtime or a version
label - either of those can agree while content differs, which is the failure being guarded.

E3 - EVIDENCE OBLIGATIONS, 7/7, scoped to the OPERATION CONTRACT and never universal. Valid evidence
permits; missing, wrong-target and wrong-revision evidence each refuse BEFORE writing, with the absence of
the write asserted by digest rather than inferred from the verdict. E3-SCOPE proves a contract declaring no
obligation is unaffected. E3-NON-VACUITY feeds the NORMATIVE token itself as evidence - right context,
wrong kind - so permission cannot launder itself into satisfying its own precondition.

AND THE POSITIVE CONTROL EARNED ITS PLACE. My first E3 fixture passed a bare { subject } literal to
observe(), which REFUSED it: "requires an observation with evidential force and complete provenance". No
evidence token ever existed. E3-MISSING PASSED ANYWAY - a refusal for the wrong reason - and only
E3-VALID's failure exposed it. Fixed by building observations through the observation() constructor, and
E3-FIXTURE now asserts the evidence is actually MINTED, so no future refusal can pass because evidence is
impossible to construct.

ONE MORE OF MINE: adding `evidence` to editAction broke E0-CONTROL, which compared action fields BY
IDENTITY - two frozen empty arrays are equal in meaning and distinct as objects. Fixed in the test by
comparing by value rather than contorting the executor to suit it. The control did its job.

THE CONTAINMENT BOUNDARY IS RECORDED IN THE SOURCE, because rejecting `..` is easy to mistake for
containment: refusing a traversing path does NOT establish filesystem containment through SYMLINKS or
WINDOWS JUNCTIONS. resolve() does not dereference them, so an in-root name aliasing an out-of-root target
is UNTESTED and outside demonstrated coverage.
