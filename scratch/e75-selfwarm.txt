Remove a redundant warm-up burn from calibrate(): min-of-N is self-warming, and the burn was self-load

BIND-1 caught a defect in my implementation and it is the same defect this module criticises elsewhere,
committed inside the fix for it.

calibrate() warmed the JIT with a 5e6 burn before measuring. That is unnecessary: A COLD RUN CAN ONLY EVER
BE THE MAX, so min-of-N with N>=2 discards it by construction. The warm-up bought nothing and cost one
core a few milliseconds on every sample, on a machine where three sessions sample constantly and two of
them abort on breach. The module's own header warns that an instrument which is a meaningful share of what
it samples is broken; mine was adding load for no measurement.

Warm-up now runs ONLY for runs < 2, the one case where min cannot save you. The test is rewritten to
assert the self-warming property rather than the warm-up behaviour, so it now pins the reason rather than
the workaround.

WORTH RECORDING WHY I MISSED IT. I reasoned "cold JIT lies high, therefore warm first" and never asked
what min-of-N already does to a high outlier. The premise was true and the conclusion did not follow from
it. That is the same shape as the diagnostic I offered 0d earlier - orphans-per-entry, derived from a
hypothesised cause and inheriting its assumption - and it is the third time today an inference of mine
held up in form while being wrong in substance.

AND THE REFERENCE REPLICATED ACROSS SESSIONS. My 1.2e7 min-of-5 = 43.3ms at 18:55:03Z scaled to 1e7 is
36.1ms. BIND-1's frozen constant is 35, from min-of-20 = 34.97ms at 18:58:43Z. Two sessions, two
implementations, three minutes apart, within 3%. BIND-1 declined to re-amend A2 on the second measurement,
correctly: changing a frozen constant because a later measurement AGREES with it is tuning, not
correcting.

16 tests, all passing.
