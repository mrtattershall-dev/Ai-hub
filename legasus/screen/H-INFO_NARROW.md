# Two hypotheses, frozen separately and narrowly (2026-09-21)

Kept apart on purpose. They may eventually collapse into one; forcing that now would be the
premature-abstraction failure this branch keeps finding.

## H-INFO (narrow form)

> If a conclusion `P` depends on a distinction erased by an evidence transformation `R`, then
> evidence available only through `R` **cannot by itself entitle** `P`.

This is a **necessary** condition. It explicitly does **not** claim that preserving the
distinction is sufficient for entitlement.

**Tautology guard.** Any test of H-INFO must define the entitlement criterion **independently of
recoverability**. If entitlement is defined as recoverability, H-INFO is true by construction
and tests nothing.

**Standing:** one discriminating result against H-RICH on one author-built construction
(a0f568c). Nothing against a naturally occurring case, and nothing against tautology.

## H-APPLIC

> A criterion's **applicability** is itself a proposition that must be established before its
> verdict is entitled.

Generated by four apparatus failures in which the consequence of a rule was evaluated while a
prerequisite for the rule to apply was never established:

    incident                   consequence evaluated        prerequisite never established
    no-op replacement          "repair succeeded"           the text was actually modified
    first D4 apparatus         "suppression observed"       the warning was reachable at all
    X1 in D-COMP-1             "F1 satisfied"               the WHOLE witness, not one clause
    first H-INFO run           "DISCRIMINATED"              the partitions actually differed

**Standing: none.** Four retrospective instances, all found by the same author, and the fourth
was found only after the pattern had been named. H-APPLIC has made no prospective prediction.

**It may or may not collapse into H-INFO.** The reading that would merge them — applicability and
verdict collapsing into one scorer state — is available, and is deliberately not adopted.

## The sharpest statement currently supported

> Not all information loss matters. The dangerous loss is the collapse of states across a
> distinction required by the proposition being inferred.

This explains why a two-valued representation can outperform a three-valued one for a particular
claim, which is what a0f568c measured.
