## What made the difference, in order of size

1. **I6 — dividing the work smaller.** Round 1 failed identically five times: the model bound the
   file's own `plantSeed` to keydown, and that function sets stage 1 and never spends a seed, so no
   wiring could satisfy the check. Making the hole the **body of one handler** — key filter, tile key
   and redraw supplied — moved code insertion from 2 of 5 to 5 of 5.
2. **I7 — a boundary the model would otherwise cross.** Round 2 inserted code every time and broke the
   page 4 times in 5, because it ran past a small hole by re-emitting the lines that follow it: 15 to
   147 lines where 4 were wanted. Cutting the infill at the first line of the suffix took the same run
   from 0 accepted to 3.
3. **The verified baseline (I5).** Movement was preserved 5 of 5 in every round here. Every earlier
   increment-2 experiment inherited a page that threw on each keypress; removing that is why an
   error-free result was reachable at all.

## The assistance ledger, and which parts are infrastructure rather than decisions

Seven things I did. **They are an assistance ledger, not seven decisions a system must make at run
time** — and separating those two is what makes step 2 tractable:

    STANDARD INFRASTRUCTURE - task-independent, no judgement, build once and always on
      I4  cut a completion at a document close
      I7  cut a completion at the first line of the suffix it was given
      I5  validate a baseline through the full required sequence before building on it
      (I2) prefer a bounded edit over a whole-file return

    JUDGEMENT - needs machinery that does not exist yet, and differs per task
      I1  choose the unit of work: which slice of the requirement to attempt now
      I3  choose how much to say and in what shape
      I6  choose WHERE the slot goes - and re-choose it when the model reuses something broken

I4, I7 and I5 are mechanical rules with no free parameters, and they can simply become part of the
harness. **I1, I3 and I6 are the real subject of step 2**, and I6 is the hardest: in round 1 the model
reused a function that could not satisfy the check, and deciding that the slot should therefore move
inward is exactly the judgement nothing in the system makes today.

**Replaying these seven decisions on this task would demonstrate automation of a known procedure, not
the choosing of guidance.** I already know the answer here: the site, the slot shape, the instruction
and the solution's structure are all in this record. A policy tuned until it reproduces them would be
fitted to a solved problem.

**So step 2 needs a fresh task**, with the policy frozen before it runs, where I supply the requirement
and the checks and **not** the edit site or the solution's structure. That is `ASSIST-2_DEFINITION.md`.

**Step 2's bar:** one fresh, independently checked addition where the system chooses the scope and
guides the model, with **zero interventions from me**, reporting its own counts against the 15

## What this establishes, and what it does not

- **Established:** this 1.5B, locally, at $0, can produce the four-line body of a planting handler
  that passes an unchanged independent gate and the stricter no-error spec, inside a slot a person
  shaped. The ceiling exists.
- **NOT established: a success rate.** Three byte-identical candidates are one solution reproduced
  three times. Two of five attempts in the same round did not pass, and no claim about how often this
  succeeds follows from five seeds on one task.
- **Established:** the earlier "no accepted handler" results were about the configurations tried, not
  about the model. **I was closer to right when I said the constraint was our apparatus and our
  specification than when I said it was the model — but only the specific claim is supported: under
  this assistance, this model does it.**
- **NOT established:** that the model would do it with less assistance, or that any of these seven
  interventions is necessary. Only the ones removed and retested would be.
- **NOT established:** anything about a second increment, an unfamiliar task, or a failure class the
  catalogue does not cover.
- **NOT autonomy, at all.** Seven human decisions produced this. That is the entire point of counting
  them, and step 2 is the test that matters.
