## R3 is closed on three independent levels

1. **No field exists.** `additionalProperties: false` on `derivation`, and there is no `requires`
   property to add. Refused at both nesting levels on the producer side.
2. **Nothing reads it.** A hand-forged certificate carrying `derivation.requires: []` past the schema
   is still refused, because the adapter resolves the requirement from its own registry. The test
   asserts the local list was the one used.
3. **The source cannot express it.** With comments stripped, the adapter contains no `requires: [`
   literal and no read of `derivation.requires`.

## The consequence I promised not to hide

**Stage B's case 3 now fails as a missing relation witness.** The scope promotion that took an

## Drift is about meaning, not spelling

`RULE_DEFINITION_MOVED` fires on a digest mismatch, and the digest covers `{name, requires sorted,
version}`. Adding a requirement moves it. **Renaming a rule with identical requirements also moves
it**, because identical names are not identical referents — the lesson `justification.mjs` recorded
when a producer coordinate named `history` silently acquired a declared dimension's authority.

## What the producer contributes, exactly

Witnesses are emitted only where the obligation record already carries the fact: `COVERAGE` where the
claimed domain is exhaustively covered, `MEMBERSHIP` where an observed element's membership in it is
established. The producer carries **fingerprints, not definitions** — `RULE_FINGERPRINTS` in
`certificate.py` is identity only, so the requirements are not duplicated across two languages, which
would be two definitions of one vocabulary.
