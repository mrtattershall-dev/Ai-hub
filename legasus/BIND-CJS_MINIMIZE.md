# BIND-CJS step 13 — squeeze the incidental differences out (frozen 2026-09-21 07:05)

Step 12 killed five deciders but showed only P5 reaching observational indistinguishability.
P1, P3 and P4's hostile and genuine bundles differed — **and on inspection those differences
were scars of how I built the worlds**, not information the evidence offers about the
proposition. This expedition removes each scar and asks reality again.

Under the corrected charter: reality does not decide what may be built, only what a
construction is entitled to claim. So the question is not "is this architecture allowed" but
"after the incidental differences are gone, does the recorded evidence still separate a world
where the proposition is true from one where it is false?"

## The scars, and their removal

| # | Step 12's incidental difference | Removed by |
|---|---|---|
| S1' | the pre-seeded request was written **before** the run, so its event rank differed | a foreign writer seeds the channel **mid-run**, at the point the genuine witness would issue |
| S3' | the hostile world had **one** execution event, the genuine **two** | the hostile witness loads twice in one process, relabelling itself `worker` for the second load — two events, two roles, one process |
| S4' | the hostile world emitted an `operation-start` record the genuine world **did not have** | both worlds emit it; and the bundle is scored **twice**, once over step 11's original field set and once with `operation-start` included |

S4' is deliberately scored under two field sets, because that is the cleanest available test of
whether a boundary belongs to the *evidence configuration* rather than to the world.

## Predictions

**N1.** With the seeding moved mid-run, S1's bundles become **identical**. P1 converts from
decider weakness to **evidence limit**: nothing recorded identifies the issuer of a request.
FALSIFIER: they still differ.

**N2.** With the counts equalised, S3's bundles become **identical**. P3 converts to **evidence
limit**: nothing recorded establishes that two role labels are two processes.
FALSIFIER: they still differ.

**N3a.** Scored over step 11's ORIGINAL field set, S4's bundles become **identical** — under
that configuration the proposition is not identifiable.
**N3b.** Scored with `operation-start` included, they **differ** — the extended configuration
carries the distinction that the original lacked.
FALSIFIER for either half: the opposite outcome.

N3a and N3b together are the point. If both hold, the boundary demonstrably **moved when the
observation configuration changed**, which is evidence that these limits are properties of what
is recorded rather than facts about the world.

## What this expedition may not do

- It may not rescue any proposition. Converting a death from "decider weakness" to "evidence
  limit" makes the claim **weaker**, not stronger, and that is the expected direction.
- It may not propose a decider, a field, an interface or a name. If N3b holds, the correct
  reading is *this configuration carries it*, **not** *therefore Legasus should record
  operation starts*. That would be a design decision earned by a different experiment.
- It may not treat a surviving difference as meaningful. A distinction that survives
  minimisation becomes the **next target**, not the answer.
- Deciders, mechanism (`cjs-preload.mjs` @ e41c1e3) and BIND's interpretation stay untouched.
- Every finding is for these constructed worlds under this observation model. Not a theorem.

## P2 is deliberately excluded

P2 was the one death that plausibly admits a stronger decider over existing fields. It gets its
own expedition, with its own hostile worlds, precisely because it is the candidate most likely
to survive repeated attack — and a proposition that survives attacks designed for other
propositions has not survived anything.
